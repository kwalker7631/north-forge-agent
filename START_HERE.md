# Start here

You already have Google. You already have a chatbot in a browser.
That is not the problem.

The problem is a tech on a live call who types **what is an F248** and gets
a paragraph that sounds smart and is wrong. They give up. The machine is
still down. That is not stupidity. That is a tool that asked them to be a
prompt engineer on a service call.

North Forge is the opposite of that.

Plug in the stick. Double-click **Start North Forge**. Type the code, the
model, what you already tried, in ordinary English. It is supposed to answer
like a senior on the desk who has seen the call before — and to say “I don’t
have that, send me the page” instead of making one up.

If you came here because you do not trust “AI,” good. Neither does this
page. Distrust is earned. A tool that hides the hole and fills it with
confidence is why people walk away. This one is built to show the hole.

It is for the person who never wants to see the word *model*.
It is for the expert who is tired of tools that move every six weeks.
It is for the book-smart person who can recite a manual and still cannot
reason through a paper path.
It is not a test. There is no prompt to memorize.

## Why Google is not enough

Google is a pile of pages. A general chatbot is a pile of pages with manners.
Neither one knows *your* fleet, *your* last three tickets, or that F248 on
*this* model is not the same story as F248 on the last one.

A usable answer on this job is short, ordered, and honest:

1. What to look at first
2. What not to touch yet
3. What evidence to grab if it is still dead
4. What we do not know yet

That takes years of compartmentalized TSC work — device, PC, network,
PaperCut, MyQ, the rest — sitting in procedures, not in an 8,000-character
prompt the tech will never write.

The 8,000-character prompt already happened. You do not have to write it.
That is the point of the stick.

## What “humanizer” means here

No “Great question.”
No fake certainty.
No essay when you asked for a code.
A voice that sounds like a person who has been on the tools, not a product
blog.

It learns what *you* just taught it. It is supposed to keep that. It is
supposed to correct itself when you catch it. That is the pocket brain:
not a genius in the cloud, a go-to on the desk that gets less wrong the
longer your shop uses it.

## How small the first step is

1. Plug the stick in.
2. Double-click **Start North Forge**.
3. Type the problem the way you would tell a coworker.
4. If you get lost, type `/menu`.

Do not format the stick. Do not “set up Python.” Do not learn a vendor.
